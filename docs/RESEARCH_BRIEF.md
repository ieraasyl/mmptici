# CQA research brief

Date: 2026-11-07. Scope: evaluator design, contact-center QA practice, evidence grounding, Kazakh handling, calibration with ~20 labels, STT for RU/KZ telephony, local-LLM option. Every claim carries a URL. Vendor numbers are marked as self-reported.

DEV facts used below (from `data/raw/dev.json`): 65 dialogs, 30 RU chats, 15 KZ chats, 15 EN chats, 5 RU voice calls; chats are 11 to 20 turns, calls up to 115 turns; KZ chats are Cyrillic Kazakh; the calls start with a garbled Kazakh IVR greeting ("Салими цбе Фридом брокер за кошки...") and a system event saying the dialog language changed from kk-KZ, so the "RU" calls contain Kazakh IVR audio transcribed as noise.

## 1. Top 10 recommendations, ranked by expected gain in GT agreement per hour

| # | Recommendation | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Turn each criterion into explicit Boolean sub-checks in the prompt (for example c01 = greeted + in client language + introduced self, all in the first operator turn) and let code combine them, instead of asking for one Likert verdict. | CheckEval: Boolean checklists raised mean human correlation by about 0.10 and cut cross-model variance 5x; gains largest for mid-size models. The organizers' rubric is already checklist-shaped. | https://arxiv.org/html/2403.18771v2 |
| 2 | Keep reasoning on (Qwen3.8 thinking), but force a fixed structure: "list client questions, list operator actions, then verdict per criterion". | G-Eval's CoT + form-filling was the first method to beat 0.5 Spearman on SummEval; Qwen3 small-model study found explicit reasoning improves judging accuracy. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16634, https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13332v1 |
| 3 | Three samples at temperature 0.6, per-criterion majority vote; do not use greedy. | Rating Roulette: majority of 3 runs beat the average single run by 1 to 3 balanced-accuracy points and greedy decoding lowered human agreement; more than 3 to 5 runs did not help. Qwen3 was the most self-consistent judge tested. | https://arxiv.org/html/2510.27106 |
| 4 | Spend labeling time on a written labeling guide with decision rules for N/A vs No per criterion, then label 20 dialogs with two labelers each and measure human-human kappa. | Protocol choice alone moved reported accuracy from 0.551 to 0.899 on a rubric benchmark; human-human agreement is the ceiling you are chasing. If your own labelers disagree on N/A, the LLM cannot match GT either. | https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.00093.md, https://arxiv.org/html/2510.27106 |
| 5 | Put the N/A conditions and the scale descriptions verbatim in the prompt and require the model to quote which condition fired. | Rubric locking and evidence-anchored scoring reduce instability and let compact models approach proprietary judges. The organizers' GT was produced by humans reading those exact descriptions. | https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08654 |
| 6 | Verify evidence in code: cited turn index must exist, be an operator turn when the criterion is about the operator, and the quote must fuzzy-match the turn text; on failure, re-ask once with the error. | Span-binding plus literal presence tests is the standard anti-fabrication pattern; cheap and deterministic. | https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/42361/46322, https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23452 |
| 7 | Report per-criterion agreement with the 2x2 table, MET rate, Cohen's kappa for binary, quadratic weighted kappa for c04/c06, plus pooled per-dialog score MAE; bootstrap over dialogs. | Pooled accuracy hides which criterion fails; kappa is undefined or misleading when a label is rare (c08 is nearly always No). | https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.00093.md, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.12661 |
| 8 | Add a second model family (gpt-oss-120b or DeepSeek on the same router) as a third voter only for criteria where Qwen's 3 samples disagree. | Panel of three small judges from different families beat a single large judge on Cohen's kappa with humans and reduced intra-model bias. Costs almost nothing on Cerebras. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796 |
| 9 | Judge KZ and EN in the original language with Russian instructions; do not machine-translate first. | Translating adds a lossy step and translationese bias is worst for low-resource languages; KazMMLU shows mid-size open models handle Kazakh at roughly Russian-minus-a-few-points, which is enough for short service chats. | https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10351, https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12829v2 |
| 10 | Skip DSPy/TextGrad. Do manual error-analysis loops on the mismatch list, with a 14/6 split so you can detect prompt overfitting. | Practitioner reports show MIPROv2 overfits at 10 to 30 examples; guides recommend 50+; the 24-hour window does not pay back the setup. | https://lady-h-s-applied-data-science-wo.gitbook.io/applied-data-science-in-my-garden/lotus-queen/prompt-optimization/experiments-with-small-dataset, https://theneuralbase.com/dspy/learn/intermediate/miprov2-vs-bootstrapfewshot-when-each/ |

## 2. Findings per research area

### 2.1 LLM-as-a-judge for rubric and checklist scoring

**What the literature says.**

- G-Eval (Liu et al., 2023): GPT-4 with chain-of-thought evaluation steps and form-filling reached Spearman 0.514 on SummEval, a large jump over prior metrics; authors flag bias toward LLM-generated text. https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16634
- CheckEval (Lee et al., 2024, v2 2025): decompose each criterion into Boolean questions, aggregate in code. Across 12 evaluator models, mean human correlation rose by about 0.10 (Mistral-Small 0.18 to 0.45, Llama3.1-8B 0.24 to 0.41, GPT-4o 0.32 to 0.50 on SummEval); inter-evaluator Krippendorff alpha rose from 0.09 to 0.48, reaching 0.67 for large models, close to the 0.7 human ceiling on SummEval. Variance across models fell 5x. Negative findings: on Topical-Chat two strong models got slightly worse; strict yes/no underperformed on low-quality texts; asking multiple questions in one prompt was no worse than one per prompt in their pilot. No CoT was used. https://arxiv.org/html/2403.18771v2
- Prometheus 2 (Kim et al., EMNLP 2024): open 7B/8x7B evaluator fine-tuned for rubric-based direct assessment; best open model on four direct-assessment benchmarks, but English-centric. Not worth deploying here: a general Qwen3 with reasoning is stronger for RU/KZ. https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01535
- Replacing Judges with Juries (Verga et al., 2024): a panel of three judges from different families (Command R, Haiku, GPT-3.5) had higher Cohen's kappa with humans than GPT-4 alone, less intra-model bias, 7x cheaper; binary tasks used max voting. https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796
- Rating Roulette (2025): LLM judges are self-inconsistent across runs (Qwen3 gave the same verdict on all three runs in only 61% of MT-Bench cases); greedy decoding reduces agreement with humans; majority of 3 runs beats a single run by 1 to 3 points; use chance-corrected metrics because accuracy 0.827 coincided with alpha 0.478 on MT-Bench. https://arxiv.org/html/2510.27106
- Bias surveys: position bias matters for pairwise comparison, which you are not doing; a 2026 reproduction finds it negligible on current models; verbosity bias is largely a position-bias artifact and frontier judges are length-robust; self-preference bias (favoring own family's text) is the one that survives, up to 16% score inflation for some models, which argues for a second family as tie-breaker. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.15594, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.05579, https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23178, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21819
- Decomposition versus holistic: a legal-domain decomposed judge aligned better with experts than holistic or multidimensional LLM scores; per-criterion agreement spreads widely (0.77 to 0.90 across criteria in one creativity study), so always report per criterion. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.16093v2, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.28465
- Model-generated rubrics are less stable than human-written ones (GER-Eval). Use the organizers' text verbatim; do not let the model rewrite it. https://arxiv.org/html/2602.08672v1
- RULERS (2026): lock the rubric, anchor each score to extracted evidence; compact models then approach proprietary judges. https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08654
- Confidence: vote share across samples is the cheap, defensible confidence signal; verbalized confidence from a single call is not calibrated (practitioner consensus, no strong paper). Treat vote share less than 1.0 as "needs review" in the UI.

**Adopt.** Boolean sub-checks per criterion, reasoning in a fixed order, 3-sample voting at T=0.6, second-family tie-break, per-criterion kappa reporting, rubric verbatim.

**Skip.** Pairwise comparison, position-swap tricks, trained evaluators (Prometheus), auto-generated rubrics, verbalized confidence as the only confidence signal.

**Per-criterion vs all-at-once.** CheckEval found no loss from batching questions in one prompt. One call for all 8 criteria keeps the shared context (the question list extracted once) and halves cost. The one exception worth a separate call is c08 (gross violations): a short dedicated yes/no call with explicit examples of "paid service outside the company" avoids the base rate problem where the model never flags.

### 2.2 Contact-center QA automation

- Amazon Connect Contact Lens: generative-AI evaluation forms (preview May 2024, auto-fill and auto-submit December 2024, Bedrock-based). Limits: up to 10 AI-answered questions per contact in auto-submit; AWS explicitly advises against AI for "highly subjective" questions such as "Was the agent attentive?". It has a calibration feature so human evaluators align on criteria, and in May 2026 extended natural-language criteria to self-service interactions. https://docs.aws.amazon.com/connect/latest/adminguide/generative-ai-performance-evaluations.html, https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2024/12/amazon-connect-contact-lens-agent-performance-evaluations-generative-ai, https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/05/amazon-connect-customer-gen-AI-evaluations-self-service
- Level AI QA-GPT: claims over 90% agreement with a consensus of human QA managers ("in line with the average QA manager") and 85% scorecard coverage; shows reasoning plus conversation quotes per score; scorecard questions are typed in natural language, separate rubrics for human and automated review. Self-reported, no methodology. https://thelevel.ai/blog/the-future-of-qa-is-here-meet-qa-gpt
- Industry baseline numbers (McKinsey-cited, secondary): automated QA above 90% accuracy versus 70 to 80% for manual scoring; one telecom case 82% accuracy at 100% coverage. Treat as marketing. https://blog.ultatel.com/automated-quality-scoring-for-smarter-qa, https://thelevel.ai/blog/ai-call-center-qa-what-buyers-need-to-know-level-ai
- Google CCAI Insights Quality AI: documentation URL moved; nothing verifiable retrieved. Not cited.
- Common structural pattern across vendors: natural-language question per criterion, answer plus quote, a human-review queue for subjective items, calibration sessions where humans score the same calls. Empathy-type questions are the acknowledged weak spot everywhere.

**Adopt.** Quote-per-score, "needs review" queue for low-confidence criteria, and a calibration page in the UI showing agreement with human labels (judges like proof of process). Treat c05 empathy as the criterion most likely to disagree with GT; bias the prompt toward the organizers' rule (politeness alone is Yes unless a voiced complaint was ignored).

### 2.3 Evidence grounding

- Pattern: the model cites a turn index plus a short quote; code checks (a) index in range, (b) speaker matches the claim type, (c) quote is a substring or fuzzy match (ratio above 0.8) of that turn; otherwise re-ask once with the failure text. Exact match catches fabrications but rejects minor paraphrase, so pair it with fuzzy matching. https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/42361/46322, https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23452
- Build citations from retrieved spans, not model-written strings: the UI should render the original turn text, never the model's quote. Store `turnIndex` plus matched char offsets. https://alpeshnakrani.com/books/hallucination-mechanically/a-citation-is-not-proof/
- "Action missing": ask for `expectedAt` as a turn span with a rule per criterion that code can validate: c01 = first operator turn; c03 = the operator turn immediately before the first disclosure or protected action; c07 = last operator turn plus the following system close event. The organizers' own example (cite last operator reply and the chat-closure event for a No on closing) matches this. Validate in code that the span contains at least one operator turn.
- N/A: require `naReason` to quote the checklist's `naCondition` text and cite the client turn that proves it (for c02, the client turn that already contained enough information; for c04, the system close event before any request). Reject N/A on criteria with `allowNA=false` in code.
- A verifier pass (second call that sees only the criterion, the verdict, and the cited turns, and must answer "does the evidence support the verdict") is cheap on Cerebras. Literature support is thin for verifiers raising agreement; its value is in catching parse-level and citation-level errors, so run it but report, do not auto-override.

### 2.4 Multilingual and Kazakh

- KazMMLU (ACL 2025): 23k questions, Kazakh and Russian. Average accuracy: GPT-4o 76.6, DeepSeek V3 76.9, Gemma-2-27B 57.4, Llama-3.1-70B 56.2, Qwen2.5-7B-instruct 47.8, Sherkala-Chat-8B 45.6, KazakhLLM-8B 41.7. Kazakh-tuned 8B models did not beat general models of the same size. English prompts beat Kazakh prompts in few-shot; models score lower on Kazakh than Russian. DeepSeek V3 collapsed under Kazakh prompting (76.9 to 45.5), so keep instructions in Russian or English, not Kazakh. https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12829v2
- Qwen3 lists Kazakh among 119 supported languages; no published Kazakh judge quality figures for Qwen3 or Qwen3.8 were found. https://dev.to/projedefteri/qwen3-hybrid-thinking-and-superior-performance-in-119-languages-proje-defteri-56d2
- Multilingual judging: consistency drops in low-resource languages; ensembles help (How Reliable is Multilingual LLM-as-a-Judge, 2025). Translationese bias is "significantly elevated" for low-resource languages (2026), which argues against translate-then-judge. CE-Judge translates everything to English via Google Translate but reports no head-to-head. https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2505.12201, https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10351, https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2507.06774v2
- Sherkala (MBZUAI/Inception, Llama-3.1-8B adapted, Feb 2025) exists but is weaker than 27B general models on KazMMLU; not useful as a judge. https://cerebras.ai/press-release/inception-and-mbzuai-launch-sherkala-transforming-the-llm-landscape-for-kazakhstan

**Adopt.** Judge in original language, instructions in Russian (the labelers' language and the checklist's language), with a one-line note that the client language is given in the `language` field and the operator must greet in it. For KZ dialogs, add a side field in the model output "short Russian gloss of the operator's turns" so reviewers who do not read Kazakh can check evidence; this is a UI feature, not an input to the verdict. Check the 15 KZ DEV dialogs first in error analysis; if KZ agreement is clearly worse, test a KZ-to-RU translation side-channel on those only.

**Skip.** Kazakh-specific LLMs, translating before judging.

### 2.5 Calibration with few labels

- Metrics: binary criteria: 2x2 table, MET rate (share of Yes), Cohen's kappa (phi and MCC coincide); ordinal c04/c06: quadratic weighted kappa with declared weights plus exact-agreement; N/A: decide in advance whether N/A is a third class or excluded, and report coverage; aggregate: per-dialog total MAE because the organizers may compare totals. Bootstrap over dialogs, not verdicts. Mark undefined kappa (c08 never Yes) as NA, not 0. https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.00093.md, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.12661
- Human ceiling: measure two-labeler agreement on at least 10 dialogs; one benchmark found judge-author weighted kappa 0.53 against 0.25 human-human. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10016
- Overfitting: with 20 labeled dialogs, keep 6 untouched until the last prompt iteration; log every prompt version with its per-criterion report; stop when the held-out score does not move.
- DSPy MIPROv2 / BootstrapFewShot: sources disagree on minimum size (20 for BootstrapFewShot, 50 to 100 for MIPROv2, one claims 1000+); a documented 30-record run overfit even with regularization. Not worth it in 24 hours. Few-shot examples hand-picked from DEV (one Yes, one No, one N/A per criterion, kept out of the eval split) are the cheap alternative and KazMMLU found few-shot helped consistently. https://theneuralbase.com/dspy/learn/intermediate/miprov2-vs-bootstrapfewshot-when-each/, https://lady-h-s-applied-data-science-wo.gitbook.io/applied-data-science-in-my-garden/lotus-queen/prompt-optimization/experiments-with-small-dataset, https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12829v2
- Error-analysis loop: for each mismatch print human label, model label, model explanation, cited turns; classify as (a) rubric ambiguity, resolve in labeling guide and prompt, (b) model missed evidence, add a sub-check, (c) labeler error, fix label. Re-run all 20 after every change (about 60 calls, under a minute on Cerebras).

### 2.7 Local LLM option

No judge benchmark (JudgeBench, RewardBench) reports Gemma 3 27B or 2026 Qwen3.x 27B/32B models for RU/KZ. Evidence that transfers: KazMMLU puts Gemma-2-27B at 57.4 versus Qwen2.5-7B at 47.8 and the Kazakh 8B models lower; Rating Roulette found Qwen3 the most self-consistent open judge tested; the small-Qwen3 study found thinking mode improves judging. https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12829v2, https://arxiv.org/html/2510.27106, https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13332v1

Recommendation: serve the same Qwen3-family dense model locally (Qwen3-32B, or the 27B dense variant of the generation you already use in the cloud, if weights are released) with vLLM in thinking mode; fall back to Qwen3-14B AWQ or Gemma-3-27B on a 24 GB card. Serving: vLLM for an OpenAI-compatible endpoint (same client code, just change base URL and model id), `--max-model-len 16384`, `--reasoning-parser qwen3`, guided JSON via `response_format`. Ollama or llama.cpp with a Q4_K_M GGUF is the laptop demo path (Gemma-3-27B Q4 fits in 20 GB, Qwen3-14B Q4 in 10 GB). Expect 10 to 40 s per dialog locally versus 1.2 s on Cerebras; present it as a toggle "local / cloud" in the UI and run the local mode on 3 to 5 dialogs during the demo. Show the model id and endpoint on the evaluation card so judges see diversity.

## 3. Evaluator design recommendation

**Structure (one dialog):**

1. Call A, main judge, Qwen3.8-27B via Cerebras, thinking on, JSON mode, temperature 0.6, n=3 (3 separate requests; Cerebras may not support `n`). System prompt in Russian: scoring philosophy from the PDF, rules (only operator turns are judged, bot and system are context, ASR noise is not a literacy fault), and the strict output schema. User message: metadata, numbered transcript `[12] operator (03:41): ...`, then the criteria rendered from `ChecklistConfig` with answer type, scale labels, N/A condition, and for each criterion the Boolean sub-checks written by the team (the replaced criterion at test time will have none, so the renderer must work without them). Required output order inside the JSON: `clientQuestions[]`, `operatorActions[]`, then per criterion `{subChecks, answer, scaleValue, evidence[{turnIndex, quote}], expectedAt, naReason}`.
2. Code: validate against Zod; check every cited `turnIndex` exists and quote fuzzy-matches (difflib ratio above 0.8) that turn; reject N/A where `allowNA=false`; clamp scale values to listed steps. On any failure, retry once with the validator message.
3. Vote: per criterion majority over 3 samples; for scale criteria take the median; `confidence = voteShare`. Keep the explanation and evidence from the sample that agreed with the majority and had the most valid evidence.
4. Call B, tie-break, only when a criterion is 2-1 or 1-1-1: `openai/gpt-oss-120b:cerebras` with the same prompt, single sample; its vote joins the pool. Expected on about 20 to 30% of criteria.
5. Call C, gross-violation check, every dialog: short dedicated prompt to the main model listing the three violation types with two examples each; Yes requires a quote. Merge with the c08 vote by OR, but store both.
6. Verifier (optional, later): for each criterion, one call with the criterion text, verdict, cited turns only; answer supports / does not support. Report in the UI as a flag; do not override.

**Cost and latency per dialog (chats, about 1.5k input tokens, 3k reasoning plus 700 output tokens per call):** 3 main calls about 14k tokens total, roughly $0.02; tie-break and gross check add about $0.005; verifier for 8 criteria about $0.01. Total about $0.03 to $0.04 per chat, 60 to 100 s wall time if sequential, about 5 s if the 3 samples and the gross check run in parallel. Voice calls (115 turns, about 6k input tokens) are about 2x. These assume the pricing in the team doc ($0.99/$1.49 per M).

**Prompt content that matters most, in order:** the eight N/A and No decision rules from the labeling guide; scale labels verbatim; the "list client questions first" step for c04; the instruction that the bot's greeting and identity check do not count as the operator's unless the organizers answer otherwise; three hand-picked few-shot mini-examples (a c02 N/A, a c07 No with system close event, a c08 Yes from p1-ru-022) kept out of the calibration split.

## 4. STT pipeline recommendation (one engineer, about 8 hours, one GPU)

Target: Russian call-center audio with Kazakh IVR and occasional Kazakh speech; output `{timestamp ISO8601, speaker: client|operator|bot|system, text}`.

1. **Ingest (30 min).** ffmpeg to 16 kHz mono WAV. If stereo with one party per channel, split channels and skip diarization entirely; channel = role (common in telephony recordings, check the organizers' files first).
2. **VAD and segmentation (30 min).** Silero VAD to drop silence, hold music, and dead air before ASR; this is the single biggest hallucination fix for Whisper-family models. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.11378
3. **Diarization (1 h).** pyannote/speaker-diarization-3.1 on GPU, `num_speakers=2` for the operator segment of the call (after the IVR). Accept the gated license, set HF token. Expect DER in the 10 to 20% range on narrowband audio; two-speaker constraint helps. https://huggingface.co/pyannote/speaker-diarization-3.1
4. **ASR (2 h).** Primary: GigaAM v3 e2e-RNNT (MIT, Russian, punctuation and normalization, word timestamps, authors claim 30% gain on call-center audio). Run per VAD segment, not longform, to avoid its pyannote longform dependency. Secondary, for segments the language-ID step flags as Kazakh: `olzhasAl/whisper-large-v3-tulpar` via faster-whisper or transformers with `language="kk"`. Language ID per segment: Whisper large-v3-turbo `detect_language` on the segment, or a cheap heuristic (ratio of Kazakh-only letters ә ғ қ ң ө ұ ү һ і in the GigaAM output is useless because GigaAM is Russian-only, so use Whisper LID). If time runs out, run Whisper large-v3 alone with `language=None` and Silero VAD, `condition_on_previous_text=False`, `no_repeat_ngram_size=3`; quality on Russian telephony is acceptable, Kazakh will be rough. https://github.com/salute-developers/GigaAM, https://huggingface.co/olzhasAl/whisper-large-v3-tulpar
5. **Merge (1 h).** Assign each ASR word to the diarization speaker with maximal overlap (WhisperX-style); group consecutive words of one speaker into turns; turn timestamp = segment start converted to ISO 8601 by adding the offset to the recording start time (from file metadata or the organizers' record; if unknown, use epoch-relative ISO with a `+00:00` zone and say so). https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.00747
6. **Role labeling (1 h).** Rules first: the IVR portion before the first two-speaker region is `bot` (DEV calls show the IVR menus as `bot` turns and DTMF "2" as `client`); DTMF-like single digits are `client`. Then one LLM call (same Qwen endpoint) over the diarized transcript: "SPEAKER_00 or SPEAKER_01 is the operator; answer with the label and the evidence turn", seeded with the opening-line heuristic that the operator introduces themselves and names the company (93.6% accuracy in the SIGDIAL study). Emit `system` events for recording start and transfer where detectable, otherwise omit. https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2023.sigdial-1.35
7. **Output and checks (1 h).** Write the transcript JSON in the DEV schema; run the evaluator on it; compare with the organizers' provided transcript for the 5 DEV calls (WER with jiwer after normalization) to get a sanity number.

Hardware: any 16 GB+ NVIDIA GPU (GigaAM v3 and pyannote are small; Whisper large-v3 needs about 10 GB fp16). Hosted fallback if no GPU: run the same scripts on a cloud GPU (RunPod/Lambda) or use a Russian cloud STT with diarization; no reviewed cloud API with documented Kazakh telephony quality was found.

Expected quality: Russian operator speech WER roughly 15 to 25% on telephony (third-party GigaAM phone figure 18.5%, flagged optimistic); Kazakh segments 20 to 30% WER with the fine-tuned Whisper; IVR text will be garbled when the IVR is Kazakh and the model is Russian, which is what the organizers' own transcript shows and the evaluator already ignores.

Risks: pyannote license gating and model download at demo time (pre-download); speaker confusion on crosstalk; Whisper hallucination on hold music if VAD is skipped; GigaAM longform mode dependency on pyannote segmentation; two Whisper variants on one GPU may exceed memory on 16 GB (load sequentially).

## 5. Risks and open questions

1. **Unknown GT metric.** If organizers compare only totals, N/A decisions dominate (N/A redistributes 15 to 20 weight). Optimize per-criterion agreement, but also track total MAE; the N/A rules are the highest-leverage prompt text. Ask the organizers questions 1 and 3 from the architecture doc at the first opportunity.
2. **Replaced criterion at test time.** The prompt renderer must handle a criterion with no team-written sub-checks, an arbitrary scale, and an arbitrary N/A rule. Rehearse with a fake criterion (for example "Operator offered a callback: Yes/No/N/A") and confirm evidence and N/A reasoning still validate.
3. **Self-consistency ceiling.** Rating Roulette shows Qwen3 changes its verdict on about 40% of borderline items across runs; the 3-vote confidence will be below 1.0 often. That is a feature for the UI (needs-review queue) but means GT agreement on subjective c05/c06 will plateau near human-human agreement. Measure your own human-human kappa to know the ceiling.
4. **Kazakh quality unknown for Qwen3.8.** No published Kazakh judge numbers. Check the 15 KZ DEV dialogs early; if KZ error rate is clearly higher, add the gpt-oss or DeepSeek tie-break for all KZ criteria rather than only on splits.
5. **Base-rate criteria.** c08 is almost always No and c02 almost always N/A in DEV; kappa will be undefined or unstable for them. Report raw confusion tables and do not tune on kappa for those.
6. **Overfitting the prompt to DEV.** 65 dialogs, 20 labels; keep 6 held out and refuse prompt edits that only help the 14.
7. **Reasoning truncation.** Thinking models need `max_tokens` at least 6000 (team measurement); on long voice calls reasoning can run longer. Set 8000 and detect truncation (empty content) as a retry condition.
8. **Cerebras rate limits** during the control-set run (10 dialogs x about 5 calls, trivial) and the demo (fine), but the full-DEV calibration re-runs (65 x 5 = 325 calls per iteration) may hit router limits; add concurrency 4 and backoff.
9. **Transcript audio.** Organizers say transcripts are provided and STT is unnecessary; the STT track is insurance and a feature for the "distinctive features" criterion. Do not let it consume evaluator or UI time.
10. **Sources disagree** on: whether majority voting helps (Rating Roulette: modestly yes; no ordinal-specific study), minimum DSPy data size (20 to 1000+), Whisper Kazakh baselines (every number is a different test set), and vendor QA accuracy claims (all self-reported). None of the disagreements change the recommendations above.
